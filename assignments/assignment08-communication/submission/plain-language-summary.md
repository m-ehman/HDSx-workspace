# Plain-Language Summary

This summary is for public health program managers who are not data specialists and are receiving a short equity briefing on middle-income adults. It looks at average body mass index (BMI) among middle-income adults aged 20 to 80. The data come from a classroom dataset built from the US National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES). Middle-income means a family income between 1.3 and 3.5 times the US poverty line. The dataset covers 11 survey waves from 1999-2000 to 2021-2023, with no 2019-2020 wave.

In this dataset, average BMI was higher in the latest survey wave than in the first. Middle-income adults surveyed in 2021-2023 had an average BMI of 30.3, compared with 29.0 for those surveyed in 1999-2000. Average BMI did not go up in every wave, though. It dipped in 2001-2002 (28.3) and again in 2011-2012 (28.9). Each average is based on more than 1,000 adults.

These results have limits that matter for how they are used. The summary is descriptive and unweighted, so it describes the people in this dataset rather than all US adults. It also does not show why average BMI differed between survey waves.

Because of these limitations, the next step is to ask for an analysis that uses the NHANES survey weights before using these results in planning. These weights adjust for how people were chosen for the survey, so the averages can represent US adults.
